## Method and evidence limits

Window: September 28–October 11, 2026, the 14 completed calendar days in America/New_York. “Daily” requires a relevant upload on each of October 5–11. Dates come from YouTube RSS publication timestamps joined by video ID to channel metadata; UnusualVideos used full video metadata timestamps because its feed failed. All usable feeds reached older than the window. Deleted/private uploads are not observable. Recheck through the app’s paginated YouTube Data API research job after adding a key.

We searched an initial pool of 14 channel names and checked four additional candidates. Names and channel IDs were matched through YouTube metadata, not video titles claiming “daily.” Preliminary relevance screening used compilation titles and duration ≥3 minutes; the three enabled alternatives also received visual sampling.

Recent opening samples (35–40 seconds each) were inspected as frame sequences for live-action content, captions, framing and cuts. Audio streams were detected; this was not a full listening/editorial review. No continuous host or advertisement was visible in those excerpts. This cannot exclude later interruptions. The StreetCan sample was also downloaded in full and rendered by the pipeline.

## Variety and overlap

UnusualVideos adds animal and everyday mishap footage to the reaction/absurdist selections seen in StreetCan and SmallCat. No obvious identical scene appeared in the three short opening samples. This is limited visual evidence, **not a quantified full-channel overlap result**. Full same-date cross-channel overlap and duplicate misses remain part of the pilot. Exact normalized audiovisual duplicates are automatically excluded within a source date and against published episodes; sampled perceptual matches are review warnings. Crops, overlays, speed changes and different cuts can evade both. Source cards expose analyzed clip yield and duplicate counts so the shortlist can change with evidence.
